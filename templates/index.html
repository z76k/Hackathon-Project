First aid

If someone has an allergic reaction

What to do before they reach a clinic or hospital. Based on standard first-aid guidance; it doesn't replace a trained first-aider or emergency services.

Emergency in the UAE: call 998 (ambulance) or 999. Say it may be anaphylaxis, a severe allergic reaction.
Mild to moderate reaction

Sneezing, runny nose, itchy or watery eyes, or an itchy rash or hives in one area.

  1. Help them move away from the trigger, like dust, an animal or a food.
  2. Rinse skin or eyes with clean, cool water if the allergen touched them.
  3. Put a cool, damp cloth on itchy skin or mild swelling.
  4. If a doctor has already advised an allergy medicine (antihistamine), they can take it as directed.
  5. Stay with them and watch closely. If any severe sign appears, treat it as an emergency.
Severe reaction (anaphylaxis): emergency

Trouble breathing or wheezing, swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, a hoarse voice, dizziness or fainting, or pale, clammy skin.

  1. Call 998 straight away.
  2. If they carry an adrenaline auto-injector (such as an EpiPen), help them use it in the outer thigh. It works through clothing.
  3. Lie them down with their legs raised. If breathing is hard, let them sit up instead. Don't let them stand or walk.
  4. If there's no improvement after 5 minutes and they have a second auto-injector, use it.
  5. If they stop breathing normally, start CPR if you know how and follow the call handler's instructions.
  6. They still need hospital care even if they feel better, because symptoms can come back.
Asthma attack (for example, from dust)
  1. Help them sit upright and stay calm. Loosen tight clothing.
  2. Help them use their reliever inhaler (usually blue) as their asthma action plan says.
  3. Call 998 if the inhaler doesn't help, they can't speak in full sentences, or their lips turn blue.

This section is general information for a student prototype, not medical training. Learn first aid from a certified course.

About AllerGuard AI

Better questions can be a helpful first step

AllerGuard AI is a student hackathon prototype. It combines AI analysis of your answers with a machine learning dust forecast trained on real JLT air data. It offers general educational insights, not medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.

AllerGuard AI Dust data: Open-Meteo (CAMS model estimates). This project is a prototype and is not medical advice.
